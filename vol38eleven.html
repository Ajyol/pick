      Some of the reflection pieces included in this section were written by students enrolled in Topics in Elementary Mathematics (MATH 367), a junior-level mathematics course for elementary school majors. This class requires 15 hours of field experience which is satisfied through participation in the After-School Achievement Program (ASAP)—a service–learning project with Tangipahoa Parish Schools and the Tangipahoa Alcohol & Drug Abuse Council (TADAC). ASAP is a free after-school tutoring program that pairs pre-service teachers with at-risk students in grades 1-6 who need extra help in math. Service-learning is a teaching strategy designed to enrich the learning experience by integrating instruction and reflection with meaningful community service. Tutoring these children allows my students to apply and enhance many of the skills, strategies, and concepts we discuss in class. Reading their weekly reflections allows me the opportunity to address any mathematical misconceptions or gaps that may surface as these future teachers are confronted with these young, inquisitive minds. As I tell my students, “There is no better way to increase (or challenge!) your understanding of a mathematical concept than to explain it to someone else!”   When our tutoring program begins, I stress to my students that our overall “Math message” for these young elementary students is … It’s fun! It’s challenging! It’s rewarding! As reflected in the following essays, it seems this message not only applies to mathematics, but also to the tutoring experience itself.     Tena Golding, Ph.D. Director, Center for Faculty Excellence   Editor’s note: Also included in this section are reflections from students who were enrolled in Dr. Cynthia Elliott’s Education 654 class.
